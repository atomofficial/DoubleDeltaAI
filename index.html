Every design tool ranks candidates by grip. The useful ranking is the gap.

Generative protein design now produces thousands of candidates and scores them on predicted binding to the target. For an identity assay that ranking is actively wrong, because the features that make a binder stick tend to make it stick to near neighbours too. Rank the same candidate set on discrimination margin — binding to the target minus binding to the closest decoy — and you select a different set of molecules.

The same binder meets the real molecule and its look-alike. What matters is not how hard it holds either one, but how far apart the two energies sit.

The field optimises ΔG. The reagent only works because of ΔΔG.

That single substitution changes which candidates survive screening, and it is the thing no existing design pipeline is built to maximise.

A reagent that programmes are required to buy.

Identity reagents are not a speculative product category. Every therapeutic antibody and biosimilar programme runs pharmacokinetic and anti-drug-antibody assays, and each of those assays needs a reagent that recognises that specific drug.

Mandated, not discretionary

No biologic reaches approval without PK and immunogenicity data. For biosimilars the comparative work is a regulatory requirement, so the reagent is a line item that cannot be cut from a budget.

Priced by incumbents already

Suppliers sell per-drug anti-idiotypic reagents today, produced through hybridoma routes that take months and depend on animals. The market exists; the production method is the bottleneck.

Recurring by construction

A reagent is specific to one drug's idiotype, so every new programme needs a new one. Revenue scales with the pipeline rather than with a single product.

The second application is why we started.

Falsified biologics now appear across every WHO region. The defence the industry relies on is provenance — serialisation, lot numbers, supply-chain custody — and provenance has already failed: all three falsified batches in the WHO alert on semaglutide had passed through the regulated supply chain, and one carried a genuine lot number. Many of those products contained relabelled insulin, which is why the harm was hypoglycaemic shock rather than an absent benefit.

A reagent that reads the molecule does not care what the box says. The same binder we sell into the laboratory becomes a lateral-flow check where the medicine is handed to a patient.

Where we actually are.

We have designs and a selection method. We do not yet have a validated assay, and we would rather say so than discover it in diligence.

    Concept formulated

    Verify a biologic's identity using a binder selected for how well it discriminates rather than how tightly it binds.

    Critical function, in silico WE ARE HERE

    Candidates designed against a target biologic and scored against a decoy panel of look-alike molecules, entirely on public structures. The claim under test is that ranking by discrimination margin selects a materially different candidate set than ranking by affinity — and we report it against published experimental base rates rather than treating a confidence score as a result.

  4. TRL 4

    Laboratory validation

    Recombinant expression and an ELISA cross-reactivity panel against the same decoys, with a partner laboratory. This is what the raise funds.
